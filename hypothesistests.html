Yulu Business Case Study: Hypothesis Testing

Dataset: bike_sharing.csv (10,886 rows)

Problem Context: Yulu experienced an unexpected revenue dip. Management needs to identify the core factors driving electric cycle demand in India.

Research Question Statistical Test Hypotheses Outcome & Business Action
Does cycle demand differ between working and non-working days? 2-Sample Welch T-Test H₀: μ_working = μ_nonworking
H₁: μ_working ≠ μ_nonworking		 Fail to Reject H₀ (p ≈ 0.226). Overall volume is identical (~190/hr), but casual riders peak on weekends and registered commuters dominate weekdays!
Does cycle demand vary significantly across seasons? One-Way ANOVA + Tukey HSD H₀: μ_spring = μ_summer = μ_fall = μ_winter Reject H₀ (p < 10⁻¹⁰⁰). Spring demand (~116) collapses compared to Fall (~234). Deploy spring marketing campaigns.
Does cycle demand differ across weather types? One-Way ANOVA + Kruskal-Wallis H₀: All weather mean counts are equal Reject H₀ (p < 10⁻⁴⁰). Bad weather halves volume (~118 vs ~205). Schedule vehicle maintenance on predictable rainy days.
Are season and weather conditions independent? Chi-Square (χ²) Test of Independence H₀: Weather is independent of Season Reject H₀ (p < 10⁻⁶). Dependent! Trap: Weather=4 has only 1 row (breaks Cochran's rule); must combine Weather 3 & 4. 
# Yulu Python Implementation Snippet
from scipy import stats
from statsmodels.stats.multicomp import pairwise_tukeyhsd

# 1. Working Day T-Test
stats.ttest_ind(df[df['workingday']==1]['count'], df[df['workingday']==0]['count'], equal_var=False)

# 2. Season ANOVA & Tukey HSD
s1, s2, s3, s4 = [df[df['season']==i]['count'] for i in [1, 2, 3, 4]]
stats.f_oneway(s1, s2, s3, s4)
pairwise_tukeyhsd(df['count'], df['season'])

1. Hypothesis Testing Foundations & Statistical Power

Alpha (α), Beta (β), and the inescapable tradeoff between false alarms and detection sensitivity.

Core Recall Statistical Power = 1 − β. It is the probability of correctly detecting a real effect when one truly exists. Aim for ≥ 80% (0.80).
Interactive Power & Error Tradeoff Simulator High Power (≥ 80%)
Type I Error (α) 5.0%
Type II Error (β) 12.3%
Statistical Power (1 − β) 87.7%
Bento Framework: Power & Error Nuances
✓
Best Application & Power Rules
  • Upfront Sample Sizing: Before launching an experiment, determine sample size so Power ≥ 80%. Underpowered tests waste traffic.
  • Lowering β Without Raising α: Only two ways: 1. Increase sample size (n), or 2. Reduce measurement noise (σ).
⚠
Type I vs Type II Severity
  • Type I (α False Alarm): Convicting an innocent person; rolling out an ineffective feature. Set strict α = 0.01 for costly decisions.
  • Type II (β Missed Detection): Letting a guilty person go free; failing to notice a life-saving medical drug.

2. Central Limit Theorem & The CI Overlap Fallacy

Why normal-based tests work on skewed business data — and why overlapping 95% CIs do NOT mean "no difference".

Deekshant's Golden Rule Regardless of raw population skew (Walmart spend, Yulu demand), the sampling distribution of the mean &xbar; becomes normal as n ≥ 30 with SE = σ / √n. And: two 95% CIs can overlap by up to 29% and still have p < 0.05!

Many engineers falsely believe: "If two 95% confidence intervals overlap, their difference cannot be statistically significant." Test it yourself! Move the means so the whiskers barely overlap, and observe that p-value is still < 0.05.

Bento Framework: CLT & Confidence Interval Properties
✓
Core Strengths & Why It Matters
  • Liberates Real-World Data: Income distributions, order values, and app latencies are almost never Gaussian. CLT guarantees that the sample mean &xbar; behaves normally anyway if n ≥ 30.
  • Predictable Error Shrinkage: Standard Error shrinks at rate 1/√n. Quadrupling your sample size halves your estimation uncertainty.
⚠
The CI Overlap Fallacy & Traps
  • Non-overlapping CIs Guarantee Significance: If two 95% CIs do not touch, p is always < 0.01 (very safe).
  • Overlapping CIs Can Still Be Significant: Because √(SE₁² + SE₂²) is strictly smaller than (SE₁ + SE₂), two CIs can overlap by up to 29% and still reject H₀ at α = 0.05! Never diagnose hypothesis tests by eye without computing the joint test statistic.

The Yulu Weather=4 Anomaly: A Masterclass in Cochran's Rule

In the Yulu Case Study, students cross-tabulate season (4 rows) and weather (4 columns). However, Weather 4 (Heavy Rain/Ice) only occurs 1 single time out of 10,886 hours! This causes expected frequencies in column 4 to drop to ≈ 0.25 (< 1), severely breaking Cochran's condition.

How to resolve in your case study:
  • Option 1 (Recommended): Combine Weather 3 ("Light Rain") and Weather 4 into a unified "Severe Weather" category.
  • Option 2: Run Chi-Square dropping Weather 4 with explicit documentation of the single-row limitation.

14. Scaler Viva & Interview Gotchas ("Golden Traps")

The top 10 questions examiners and interviewers ask to test true conceptual mastery.

1. Never Say "Accept H₀"

Failure to reject H₀ only means you lack sufficient evidence to disprove it under current sample noise. "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."

2. The CI Overlap Fallacy

Two 95% Confidence Intervals can overlap by up to 29% and their difference can STILL be statistically significant (p < 0.05). Why? Because SE_diff = √(SE₁² + SE₂²) ≤ SE₁ + SE₂.

3. The Data Leakage Crime

Never run scaler.fit(df) or imputer.fit(df) on the whole dataset. Always split into train/test first, fit on train only, and transform test!

4. The Dummy Variable Trap

When One-Hot Encoding a category with k levels for Linear Regression, always set drop_first=True (k-1 columns), or multicollinearity causes matrix singularity!

5. Diagnostic P-Value Reversal

In diagnostic tests (Shapiro-Wilk, Levene), H₀ is what you WANT (data is normal, variances equal). Thus, you want p > 0.05 to proceed with parametric tests!

6. Large Sample P-Value Delusion

With 500,000 rows (Walmart), even a $0.05 spend difference gives p < 0.001. Always report effect sizes (Cohen's d or Cramér's V) to prove economic significance!

7. Cochran's Condition Violation

Chi-Square requires all expected frequencies E_ij ≥ 1 and ≤ 20% < 5. In Yulu, Weather=4 had only 1 observation. If unhandled, the test is mathematically invalid.

8. The A/B Test Peeking Trap

Checking p-values continuously and stopping the experiment early as soon as p < 0.05 inflates Type I false alarm rate from 5% to over 30%! Always wait for the pre-calculated sample size.
